Which subjects go missing matters more than how many

At the same removal volume, dropping the least recoverable subject pronouns pushes a model toward null subjects far harder than dropping the most recoverable ones. The effect holds on a RoBERTa rater that is free of the BERT contraction artifact, and there it is larger.

Metric: end-state overt-subject preference (length-normalized mean log-prob, overt vs null), mean of the last 3 of 97 checkpoints, on 576 condition-matched test pairs. One run per cell (h0).

The effect is subject-specific

Subject drop, with and without agreement, separates the arms most. Conjunction and expletive items show the same ordering, weaker. Object drop and extraction sit at ceiling in every arm and decile, so the manipulation does not leak into how models treat objects.

Control items behave correctly

Overt preference falls to about 0.1–0.2 for every model, including the all-removed one. These are contexts where the null form is the grammatical one, so this is not an effect of the manipulation.

An early-training transient

Around 5×10⁷ tokens (about step 200) every category converges near a peak and then separates. It is a training phase, not a decile effect, and worth knowing about when reading the log axis.

Point-to-point noise

With 72 items per category, each checkpoint value has a standard error of about 0.05. The end-state numbers above average the last 3 checkpoints over all 576 pairs.

Open question

The enrich all-removed anomaly lives in agreement items

With every pronoun removed, enrich still prefers overt subjects at about 0.6 on subject-drop items whose verb carries agreement, but about 0.28 on the no-agreement items. Baseline and lemmatize sit near 0.1 on both. The high enrich anchor (0.42 end state) is therefore about how the model treats verbs with the invented agreement suffixes. Next step: break it out by item condition (subj_1sg, 2sg, 3sg, …), since the suffixes differ by person.

Pronoun surprisal under the interventions

How each intervention changes a pronoun's circumstances

We took the same sampled subject pronouns (about 100,000) and scored each one twice: once in its baseline context, once in the intervention's edited context. The pairing is per instance, so every difference belongs to one pronoun in one sentence. We ran this with both raters: RoBERTa ±250, the current selection rater, and BERT 250:1, the retired one.

Validation: rescoring the baseline through the intervention pipeline reproduces each rater's original scores (RoBERTa: max difference 0.124 nats, ρ = 0.99998; BERT: max 0.155, ρ = 0.999999).

The contraction artifact is gone

Under RoBERTa, contracted pronouns shift the same direction as the rest, by small amounts (lemmatize +0.16, enrich +0.11). Under BERT they dropped by 2.70 and 1.47 nats, because un-contracting exposed the verb the rater could not see before.

Every intervention costs a little predictability

With RoBERTa all four changes are zero or positive, and small: most pronouns are near-certain with ±250 tokens of context. Lemmatize costs the pronouns English agreement helped most: 3rd person +0.08 vs 1st +0.03; with the verb edited, it +0.14, you +0.13, he and she +0.07–0.08.

Enrich confuses RoBERTa much less than BERT

With the verb edited, he and she rise +0.03 under RoBERTa vs +0.36 under BERT, and it +0.12 vs +0.76. RoBERTa is still a pretrained English reader, so enrich measures what it can recover from invented forms, not what a learner of the enriched corpus can.

Reshuffling is concentrated in near-ties

RoBERTa's middle deciles are separated by hundredths of a nat, so tiny shifts reorder them (17–20% stay put under lemmatize and enrich). The end deciles hold better: about half of decile 0 and 56–60% of decile 9 keep their place. Impoverish case moves pronouns by a median 0.008 nats yet reorders the middle, which is the same tie effect.

Design implication

Baseline-ranked deciles carry over cleanly for two interventions, approximately for two

The study ranks pronouns once on the baseline corpus and reuses those deciles in every intervention corpus. That holds for remove expletives (97% of decile 0 and 94% of decile 9 stay) and reasonably for impoverish case (63% / 85%). Under lemmatize and enrich only about half of each end decile would be chosen again by a ranking made inside that corpus. The alternative is ranking within each intervention corpus, at the cost of the arms no longer removing the same pronoun instances across interventions.

What this analysis turned up

The BERT 250:1 rater misjudged contracted pronouns

Its right window is one BERT wordpiece, and BERT splits "they've" into they / ' / ve, so for a contracted pronoun the rater saw only the apostrophe. Contracted pronouns scored 4.99 nats vs 1.85, and the deciles sorted partly on contraction. This is why the study moved to the RoBERTa ±250 selection (decision D14).

Status and caveats

What this is and isn't yet

  • One seed per cell. The consistency across cells makes noise an unlikely explanation, but error bars come from the full-architecture wave with more seeds.
  • Six RoBERTa least-recoverable cells are still training (gaps at 10% baseline, 80–90% lemmatize, 10% enrich). These runs were relaunched after a node failure.
  • No random remove-expletives runs exist in the pilot, so that intervention is plotted without a random row and left out of the end-state table.
  • Both surprisal raters are pretrained on ordinary English. They measure what an English reader can recover from each edited corpus, not what a model reared on it learns to use. Raters trained on each intervention corpus would close that gap.
  • Still needed: a decile balance table (person, clause type, position, genre), an item-level check of whether the arm effect tracks each test item's own recoverability, and the gpt2_large, bert_large and lstm wave on the RoBERTa selection.
