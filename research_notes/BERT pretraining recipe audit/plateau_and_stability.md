# MLM loss plateaus and post-LN vs pre-LN stability: literature evidence applied to a 24-layer post-LN BERT that stalled at train loss ~5.6-6.1

*Reading conventions.* "Cited Findings" are things a source states or shows. "Inferences" are my own reasoning from those findings and from the failure-case numbers supplied in the assignment; they are not established by the papers. Sources marked **[full text]** were read as full text (PDF converted locally, figures inspected where noted); sources marked **[abstract only]** were checked at abstract/summary level only; any detail attributed to those comes from an automated page summary and was not independently verified. Figure values marked "(by eye)" were read off a rendered figure, not from a table. Failure-case numbers (Run A, Run B, 4L/256 run) are from the assignment, not re-measured.

Failure case shorthand used below: Run A = peak LR 3.75e-5, 976 warmup steps, linear decay over 14,820 steps, batch 256x1000, ~3.8B tokens, final loss 5.62. Run B = peak LR 1e-4, 6% warmup, 20k steps, batch 256x512, ~2.6B tokens, loss ~6.1 flat from step 4k. Both: 24-layer post-LN BertForMaskedLM, hidden 1024, AdamW eps 1e-6, fp16 GradScaler, grad clip 1.0.

## Key Question 1: What is the MLM loss plateau / sudden-drop phenomenon, what has the model learned on the plateau, what triggers escape, and how do duration and escape depend on depth, LR, warmup, sequence length, dropout?

### Takeaway
The best-documented MLM case (Chen et al., ICLR 2024, BERT-base, LR 1e-4, 10k warmup, batch 256x512) shows a loss plateau near ~6 nats followed by an abrupt drop at roughly 20-30K steps (~2.6-3.9B tokens), coinciding with the sudden formation of syntactic attention heads; the general mechanism in the wider literature is slow learning of attention maps (small query/key gradients, collapsed token representations). No MLM paper I found measures how plateau length scales with depth, LR, warmup, sequence length or dropout, so those dependencies are largely undocumented for BERT-style models.

### Cited Findings

**Chen et al. 2024, "Sudden Drops in the Loss" [full text; Fig. 1 inspected]**
- Setup: uncased BERT-base (12 layers, 768 wide), AdamW, LR 1e-4, 10,000 warmup steps, batch size 256, fixed sequence length 512 (explicitly a departure from original BERT), 15% masking, BookCorpus + Wikipedia, HF transformers 4.12.5; only checkpoints before numerical instability (<=300K steps) analysed — [Chen et al. Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- The loss curve shows "a precipitous drop" that the paper states "consistently" spans 20K-30K timesteps across random seeds — [Chen et al. Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- Reading Fig. 1a by eye: loss falls from ~10.4 to ~7 within the first few hundred steps, creeps down to ~6 and stays on a plateau of ~6.0 until ~2.0x10^4 steps (the "structure onset" marker sits at ~5.9), then falls to ~3.3-4 by ~2.5x10^4 and ~2.6 by ~3x10^4 — [Chen et al. Fig. 1a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- At 256x512 = 131,072 tokens/step, 20K steps = 2.6B tokens and 30K steps = 3.9B tokens (arithmetic on the paper's stated batch/sequence settings).
- What changes at the drop: attention-derived dependency-parse accuracy (UAS) sits at ~0.05-0.09 through the plateau and jumps to ~0.3-0.4 at the structure onset (~2x10^4 steps); BLiMP accuracy sits ~0.5-0.6 (by eye) and rises to ~0.7-0.8 shortly after (the "capabilities onset") — [Chen et al. Fig. 1b,c, Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- The loss drop is described as the composition of two consecutive transitions: Syntactic Attention Structure (SAS) forms first, then grammatical capabilities appear; SAS was shown necessary for complex grammatical capabilities via a regulariser that suppresses it (Sec. 4.2) — [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- Competing strategy: when SAS is suppressed, the loss shows an earlier break at ~6K steps ("alternative strategy onset"); the paper's evidence (Appendix M) is that this strategy uses long-range/semantic n-gram-like context rather than local syntax, with attention that is less tied to relative position — [Chen et al. Sec. 4.2, App. M](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- Briefly suppressing SAS (e.g., first 3k steps) improved later quality and speed; releasing the regulariser during a transition produced the worst outcome; the authors call the transition a possible critical learning period — [Chen et al. Sec. 4.3, 5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- The transition is not an artifact of the LR schedule: warmup ends at 10K steps and the transition happens well after; the phase transition persists with continuous metrics and with weight norm on the x-axis — [Chen et al. App. H, Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- Seeds: three seeds differ in how stable UAS is after onset, but all show SAS emerging in a brief window; across 25 MultiBERTs seeds, SAS metrics are not significantly correlated with final capabilities — [Chen et al. Fig. 1 caption, App. E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- MultiBERTs (public BERT-base runs, 5 with intermediate checkpoints) show a UAS spike followed by a plateau "within 20K timesteps"; checkpoints are too coarse to localise the break more precisely — [Chen et al. App. F](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- Paper inconsistency worth knowing: Table 1/Table 2 captions say 100K steps is "~13M tokens" and 300K steps "~39M tokens"; at 131k tokens/step these should be ~13B and ~39B (looks like a units typo) — [Chen et al. Tables 1-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)

**Related abrupt-learning / plateau work**
- Gopalani & Hu (NeurIPS 2025), mostly 1-2 layer transformers on algorithmic tasks, Adam LR 1e-4 constant: during the plateau the model learns a partial solution (easier tokens only), shows strong repetition bias in outputs, and its hidden states for different tokens become nearly parallel (cosine similarity often >0.9); slow learning of the optimal attention map is identified as the bottleneck; intervening directly on the attention map (scaling attention toward the optimal pattern by c>1) shortens the plateau and reduces collapse, while c<1 prolongs it — [Gopalani & Hu, Abstract, Sec. 1, Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688) [full text]
- Same paper: representation collapse and repetition bias also appear very early in pre-training of Pythia (14M, 1B, 1.4B, 2.8B) and OLMo-2 7B; average cosine similarity of last-layer hidden states is ~0.4-0.65 at initialization but rises above 0.9 within a few training steps; greedy outputs are often trivial repetitions of a single token such as newline — [Gopalani & Hu, Sec. 6, Fig. 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688) [full text]
- Same paper: smaller initialization scale (alpha<1) gives a longer plateau with cosine similarity pinned near 1.0 in their toy setting (Fig. 36); the authors report the phenomena persist across SGD/Muon, learning rates, LR schedules, batch size, init scale and weight decay (App. G) — [Gopalani & Hu, Fig. 36, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688) [full text]
- Gopalani & Hu note this differs from rank collapse at initialization (Dong; Noci) in that their collapse emerges after a few training steps, in shallow models with linear attention — [Gopalani & Hu, Sec. 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688) [full text]
- Hoffmann et al. ("Eureka-moments", 2023): transformers on two-step tasks plateau (for hundreds of epochs in the synthetic tasks) and then suddenly learn the intermediate task; they trace this to softmax attention producing very small gradients for key/query weights, which a larger learning rate cannot fix because other gradient components stay large; fixes are a normalized softmax (NormSoftmax) or a temperature schedule starting low ("Heat Treatment"). Warmup alone and weight decay did not help — [Hoffmann et al., Sec. 1, 2, 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12956) [full text, partly read]
- Same paper, real-data check: a 12-layer RoBERTa (batch 84, LR 5e-5, Wikipedia) shows a plateau followed by a sharp drop, and NormSoftmax makes the drop come earlier (Fig. 1c, Fig. 9, App. A.18); the step at which the drop occurs was not extracted — [Hoffmann et al., Fig. 9, A.18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12956)
- Same paper's related-work reading: both attention-entropy collapse and near-uniform attention can yield small gradients; they state that before a Eureka-moment attention is in neither extreme but allocated to the wrong tokens — [Hoffmann et al., Sec. 1 "Unstable gradients"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12956)
- Belrose et al. (2024): networks learn low-order moments of the data before higher-order ones ("distributional simplicity bias"), with evidence in LLMs — [Belrose et al. abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04362) [abstract only]
- Chen et al. cite Choshen et al. (2022) for LMs behaving like n-gram models early in training and diverging later — [Chen et al. App. C.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311) (secondary citation; Choshen not read)

**Dependence on depth / LR / warmup / sequence length / dropout (what exists)**
- Depth (post-LN, translation not MLM): in IWSLT'14 De-En encoder-decoder training with Adam (LR 7e-4, inverse-sqrt, 6,000 warmup updates, betas 0.9/0.98 per the appendix table), post-LN models of depth 12 and 24 stay at ~9 nats (the figure's "random prediction" line) throughout the ~11k steps shown, while depth 6 and 8 descend (to ~3 and ~4.5, by eye); a lower row of the same figure (apparently the depth-scaled-residual variant; row label not verified) shows all depths training. The text says escaping the vanishing-gradient region "is harder in deeper nets" — [Noci et al. Fig. 1, Sec. 3.1, App. Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03126) [full text; figure inspected]
- Warmup: lengthening warmup from 8k to 16k/24k/32k updates did not rescue an 18-layer post-LN encoder-decoder — [Liu et al. 2020, Sec. 5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08249) [full text]; in Hoffmann et al.'s tasks warmup alone did not help — [Hoffmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12956)
- Warmup in BERT-large-style pre-LN training: a 0% warmup configuration diverged in Izsak et al.'s search, and configurations that had not reached validation loss <=6.0 after 3 hours were pruned — [Izsak et al. Sec. 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07705) [full text]
- LR: post-LN BERT-base with 10k warmup trains at LR 1e-4 but diverged at 3e-4 — [Xiong et al. Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745) [full text]. A post-LN variant of a 12-layer BERT-style model under an aggressive recipe (peak LR 1e-3, batch 4096, no dropout, sequence length 128, mixed precision) finished at MLM loss 7.54 with chance-level downstream accuracy (MNLI-m ~32-35%) while the pre-LN version reached ~1.9 — [Geiping & Goldstein, Tables 5 and 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14034) [full text]
- Sequence length: GPT-2 pre-training instability (loss spikes/divergence) was strongly tied to long sequences early in training via extreme Adam-variance values; a seq-128 run with large batch/LR was stable, and a sequence-length warmup stabilised 8x batch / 4x LR (GPT-2) — this is for causal LMs and measures spikes, not plateaus — [Li et al. 2022, Sec. 3-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06084) [full text]. Original BERT trained 90% of steps at length 128 and the last 10% at 512 — [Devlin et al. App. A.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805) [full text]; LTG-BERT (100M-word BNC) did the same — [Samuel et al. Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09859) [full text, partly read]; RoBERTa deliberately trained only full-length 512 sequences — [Liu et al. 2019, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692) [full text]. Chen et al.'s BERT-base used fixed 512 from step 0 and still escaped at ~20K steps — [Chen et al. Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311). A block-size curriculum for BERT is reported to improve convergence speed and downstream performance in low-resource experiments (a search-result summary lists WikiText-2 among the datasets; not verified) — [Nagatsuka et al., RANLP 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.ranlp-1.112) [abstract only, via search summary; the page fetch failed]
- Attention guidance: an auxiliary loss pulling attention heads toward patterns seen in trained models is reported to give faster convergence for bidirectional-transformer self-supervised training — [Deshpande & Narasimhan, EMNLP Findings 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02399) [abstract only]
- Dropout: I found no source that varies dropout and reports plateau length. Geiping & Goldstein disable dropout during single-epoch cramming pre-training to maximise effective updates and found no downside in that regime — [Geiping & Goldstein Sec. 4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14034) [full text]

### Inferences
- **Our runs sit exactly where Chen's BERT-base was still on the plateau.** Chen's per-step regime (256x512 tokens, LR 1e-4) is identical to Run B's; Chen's plateau level (~6.0 by eye) is very close to Run B's ~6.1; and Chen's escape (~20-30K steps) begins where Run B stopped (20k steps). Run A (14.8k steps) stopped even earlier in steps. The parsimonious reading is that both runs were truncated inside a plateau rather than permanently stuck — but this cannot be confirmed from the literature alone, because (i) Chen's model is 12 layers and ours 24, (ii) Chen's tokenizer/vocabulary differs from ours (loss levels are not strictly comparable; our unigram cross-entropy is 7.42 nats, and Chen's unigram baseline is not reported), and (iii) the plateau "level" is read by eye.
- **Differences that could push our escape later than Chen's**: depth (24 vs 12; Noci shows depth lengthening/preventing escape in post-LN translation models, though at much higher LR); Run A's LR is ~2.7x lower than Chen's and decays to zero by step 14,820. A crude "integrated learning rate" heuristic (sum of LR over steps) gives roughly 1.5 LR-steps for Chen up to step 20K (10k-step ramp + 10k at 1e-4), ~1.0 for Run B (assuming linear decay to zero at 20k), and ~0.28 for Run A; no paper tests whether escape tracks cumulative LR, so this is only a way to see that Run A was far from "equivalent" progress. Decay-to-zero schedules (both runs) also shrink the step size exactly in the window where an escape would be expected.
- **Differences that could pull escape earlier**: Run B warmup is short (1.2k steps, ramp 8.3e-8 LR/step vs BERT's 1e-8), which may help escape (more steps at peak LR) or hurt (larger early updates; see Q2).
- The 56% "the" prediction and top guess "the" for "the cat sat on the [MASK] ." match the "repetition bias + collapsed representations" description of plateau states in Gopalani & Hu and the unigram/low-order-statistics-first picture in Belrose et al. and Choshen et al.; this is a qualitative match only.
- Seq length 1000 (Run A) is longer than anything tested in the MLM-plateau literature (512 in Chen); with softmax over 1000 keys at init, attention is closer to uniform (more token-averaging), which by Noci's analysis (query/key gradient scales with (1-rho)^2, where rho is token correlation; Eq. 18) would, if anything, worsen early query/key gradients — inference, not tested by Noci for n=1000.

### Gaps
- No MLM paper reports plateau duration as a function of depth, peak LR, warmup length, dropout, or sequence length; Chen's only manipulations are the SAS regulariser and step counts. The depth evidence (Noci) is translation, not MLM.
- Step count of the Hoffmann et al. RoBERTa drop (Fig. 9) was not extracted.
- No published loss curve for a 24-layer post-LN BERT/RoBERTa at LR ~1e-4 showing when its plateau ends (BERT and RoBERTa papers report only final results/hyperparameters), so the escape step for our exact architecture is unknown.
- Chen's unigram baseline and vocabulary-specific loss floor are not given, so "6.0 vs our 6.1" is a rough match, not a controlled comparison.
- Dropout's effect on plateau escape: no source found.

## Key Question 2: What does the post-LN vs pre-LN stability literature document (failure modes, depth thresholds, warmup dependence), and is a 24-layer post-LN BERT at LR 3.75e-5 to 1e-4 expected to stall?

### Takeaway
Deep post-LN training failure is well documented, mostly in translation encoder-decoders (failures at 12-18+ layers at LR ~7e-4), with competing mechanistic explanations (large gradients near the output, "amplification" of updates, exploding early model updates followed by vanishing gradients through LayerNorm, rank collapse of tokens). There is no evidence that a 24-layer post-LN encoder at LR 1e-4 is expected to fail outright: that is the original BERT-large/RoBERTa-large regime, though trained with much longer warmup and far more steps; what the literature predicts is sensitivity to LR/warmup and a slow start, not a guaranteed stall.

### Cited Findings
- Xiong et al. (2020), Theorem 1 (mean-field, at initialization): for post-LN the gradient norm of the last FFN layer is O(d sqrt(ln d)) independent of the number of layers L, whereas for pre-LN it is O(d sqrt(ln d / L)); measured expected gradient scale grows with layer index in a 6-6 post-LN model and is flat across layers in pre-LN; the authors argue this large gradient near the output is why post-LN needs LR warmup — [Xiong et al. Sec. 3, Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745) [full text]
- Xiong et al., BERT-base experiments: post-LN with 10k warmup and LR 1e-4 trains; post-LN at 3e-4 diverged; pre-LN with LR 3e-4 and no warmup trains and is faster. The text states a "40% speed-up" and that the 10k warmup is far less than the ~200k-update acceleration, but its example sentence assigns 500k updates to Post-LN and 700k to Pre-LN, which reads as transposed relative to the claim (check Fig. 5a before quoting numbers) — [Xiong et al. Sec. 4.1-4.2, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745) [full text]
- Liu et al. (2020, Admin): unbalanced gradients are "not the root cause"; they identify an amplification effect: heavy dependency of each layer's output on its residual branch amplifies small parameter updates into large output changes; Post-LN has the heavier dependency, which destabilises early training, while too-light dependency (Pre-LN) limits final quality. 6-layer post-LN converges but 18-layer post-LN diverges (encoder-decoder NMT). Theorem/appendix: gradient vanishing at initialization occurs only through post-LN decoders' encoder-attention sub-layers; post-LN encoders do not vanish at initialization (lower layers have larger gradients). Fixing vanishing gradients alone did not stabilise training. Longer warmup (8k to 16k/24k/32k) did not rescue 18-layer post-LN — [Liu et al. Sec. 3-5, App. A.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08249) [full text]
- Liu et al. (2020, RAdam): warmup's role is attributed to problematically large variance of Adam's adaptive learning rate early in training — [Liu et al., RAdam abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03265) [abstract only]
- Wang et al. (2022, DeepNet): in 18L-18L post-LN on IWSLT-14, the model shows an exploding update at the very start followed by almost no update, i.e., it gets stuck in a poor optimum; LayerNorm input norms grow, and since the gradient through LN scales like sqrt(d)/||x|| this produces vanishing gradients that make escape hard; warmup and better initialization both mitigate. They argue exploding gradients in deep layers are not the root cause — [Wang et al. Sec. 3, Fig. 3-4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00555) [full text]
- Takase et al. (2022, B2T): post-LN with ten or more layers is "often unstable, resulting in useless models" (18L-18L WMT En-De fails to train, Fig. 1); at the start of training the decoder-side gradient norms decay exponentially toward shallower layers, and the drops in gradient norm occur at the LayerNorm parts, so they identify LN in post-LN as the main source of vanishing gradient; post-LN also preserves larger gradient norms in higher layers, which they link to better performance in shallow (<=6 layer) models — [Takase et al. Abstract, Sec. 1, 3, Fig. 3-4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00330) [full text]
- Zhang & He (2020, progressive layer dropping) on BERT: for post-LN BERT with depth L = 12, 24 and 48, the weight-gradient norm shrinks toward lower layers (their Fig. 1), whereas pre-LN keeps it roughly constant; they report post-LN is more sensitive to hyperparameters and training often diverges with more aggressive LR — [Zhang & He Sec. 3.1, Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13369) [full text]
- Noci et al. (2022): in post-LN transformers, tokens' representations become more aligned at initialization as depth grows (rank collapse in the extreme); rank collapse makes query/key gradients vanish at initialization (Theorem 3.2); the query gradient norm scales with (1-rho)^2 for token correlation rho (Eq. 18); in IWSLT'14 post-LN models of depth 12 and 24 did not train within the ~11k steps shown while depth 6 and 8 did; depth-dependent scaling of residual branches prevents the alignment — [Noci et al. Sec. 3, Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03126) [full text]
- Dong et al. (2021): without skip connections or MLPs, self-attention output converges doubly exponentially with depth to a rank-1 matrix (token uniformity); skip connections and MLPs counteract this — [Dong et al. Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03404) [full text, partly read]
- Noci et al. also report that finite-depth skip connections do not prevent token alignment from growing with depth unless residual branches are scaled — [Noci et al. Sec. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03126)
- Emadi (2026 preprint; single author, small scale, early training only): 774M-parameter GPT-2-style (36-layer) post-LN, FineWeb, LR 3e-4, 500 warmup steps, batch 64, AdamW (0.9, 0.95), eps 1e-8, three seeds, 2000 updates: pre-LN reaches ~4.51 in all seeds; post-LN splits, one seed to 4.98 and two plateauing near 7.67 from ~update 250; in the plateaued runs per-block gradient norms are <=7e-6 across layers 0-33 versus ~0.04 at layer 35, and the plateaued runs' attention is near the uniform baseline. The author states these are not converged-performance claims — [Emadi, Sec. 5 and App. D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18849) [full text]; weak evidence (very recent, uncited, single-author preprint), but it is the only source I found that reports a post-LN stall at a near-constant-predictor loss together with measured vanishing lower-layer gradients
- Counter-evidence on trainability of 24-layer post-LN BERTs: RoBERTa-large (24 layers, hidden 1024, FFN 4096, 16 heads, post-LN) was trained at peak LR 4e-4, 30k warmup steps, batch 8k, 500k steps, Adam beta2 0.98, eps 1e-6, no gradient clipping — [Liu et al. 2019, Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692) [full text]; original BERT-large used LR 1e-4, 10k warmup, batch 256x512, 1M steps — [Devlin et al. App. A.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805) [full text]. RoBERTa notes training was very sensitive to Adam epsilon and that beta2 = 0.98 improved stability at large batch — [Liu et al. 2019, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)
- Megatron-LM: BERT-style models larger than BERT-large degraded or became unstable with the original LayerNorm/residual ordering; rearranging them (their Fig. 7b, a pre-LN-like arrangement) eliminated the instability and lowered training loss — [Shoeybi et al. Sec. 5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053) [full text]
- Pre-LN is not strictly better: pre-norm degraded performance in a high-resource WMT'14 En-De setting while enabling warmup-free training with large LR in low-resource settings — [Nguyen & Salazar abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05895) [abstract only]; pre-LN has the opposite gradient mismatch (early layers get much larger gradients) and NormFormer's extra norms address it — [Shleifer et al. abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09456) [abstract only]; on 100M-word BNC pre-training both post-norm and pre-norm BERT were substantially worse than a NormFormer-like variant and "less stable" — [Samuel et al. Sec. 7.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09859) [full text, partly read]
- Pre-LN BERT-large with LR up to 2e-3 and 6% warmup was the best configuration found in a 24-hour budget; pre-LN also "allows" aggressive LR schedules, and replacing it with the original post-LN architecture under the same aggressive schedule failed (MNLI ~35%) — [Izsak et al. Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07705) [full text]; [Geiping & Goldstein Sec. 4.2, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14034) [full text]

### Inferences
- **Depth thresholds in the literature are for NMT encoder-decoders at much higher LRs (~7e-4) than ours**; BERT-base post-LN (12 layers) trains at 1e-4. Nothing I found establishes a depth/LR boundary for encoder-only post-LN BERT, so "24-layer at 1e-4 should stall" is not an established prediction; "24-layer post-LN at 1e-4 is a known-working BERT-large recipe but only with a 10k warmup and ~50x more steps than Run B" is.
- **Sources conflict on whether post-LN encoders suffer lower-layer gradient vanishing**: Liu et al. derive no vanishing for post-LN encoders at initialization (NMT, activation gradients), whereas Xiong (Fig. 3 trend), Zhang & He (BERT weight gradients), Takase (decoder side) and Emadi (trained, GPT-style) report smaller gradients in lower layers. These measure different quantities (activation vs parameter gradients; initialization vs after some training; encoder vs decoder), so they are not strictly contradictory. DeepNet's story reconciles them: a large early update raises LN input norms, and gradients then vanish dynamically after init.
- Run B's warmup ramp (6% of 20k = 1,200 steps to 1e-4, ~8.3e-8 per step) is ~8x steeper than original BERT's (1e-8 per step) and ~6x steeper than RoBERTa-large's (4e-4/30k = 1.3e-8 per step). Under the Xiong/Liu/DeepNet accounts a steep ramp on a 24-layer post-LN model is the setting where early over-large updates are most plausible. Run A's ramp (3.75e-5/976 = 3.8e-8 per step) is gentler but still ~4x BERT's. No paper quantifies a safe ramp rate for 24-layer post-LN.
- Pre-LN, DeepNorm/Admin-style init, and B2T-style connections all address the same documented post-LN weakness from different angles; pre-LN has the strongest empirical support specifically for BERT-style MLM at scale (Xiong BERT-base, Izsak BERT-large, Cramming, Megatron).

### Gaps
- No published training curve or plateau-length measurement for 24-layer post-LN BERT-large/RoBERTa-large in the early phase (first 20-50K steps).
- No paper isolates depth and LR for encoder-only post-LN MLM; the depth thresholds (>=10-12 layers fail) come from translation.
- Whether Xiong's Figure 5 statement about 500k vs 700k updates is transposed in the text could not be resolved from the extracted text.
- Emadi (2026) has not been replicated or peer-reviewed as far as I could tell.

## Key Question 3: Does the observed signature (flat loss for 15k+ steps, tiny gradients inferred from fp16 loss scale reaching 2^23, unigram-like predictions) match a specific documented failure mode: attention/rank collapse, entropy collapse, or post-LN vanishing gradients?

### Takeaway
The signature is most consistent with (a) a plain Chen-style plateau that had not yet ended, and/or (b) the "small query/key gradient, collapsed/uniform-token-representation" family (Noci, Dong, Gopalani, Hoffmann) possibly combined with post-LN lower-layer gradient vanishing (DeepNet, Takase, Zhang & He, Emadi); it does not match attention-entropy collapse (Zhai), which is documented as high-gradient-norm oscillation/divergence. The fp16 loss-scale observation is much weaker evidence of "tiny gradients" than it looks.

### Cited Findings
- Entropy collapse (Zhai et al. 2023) means pathologically low attention entropy (highly concentrated attention) accompanied by oscillating loss or divergence and slow convergence, often triggered by too-high LR or too-short warmup; the authors contrast it with rank collapse: under entropy collapse the attention matrix stays high-rank and tends to produce high gradient norms, not vanishing ones — [Zhai et al. Abstract, Sec. 2-3, Fig. 1, Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06296) [full text]
- Rank collapse (token representations perfectly aligned) causes vanishing query/key gradients at initialization (Noci Theorem 3.2), is aggravated by depth, and is harder to escape in deeper nets — [Noci et al. Sec. 1, 3.1, Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03126) [full text]
- Hoffmann et al.: near-uniform attention (many small attention scores) and too-concentrated attention can both produce small softmax gradients; before an abrupt learning moment their models' attention was in neither extreme but on the wrong tokens — [Hoffmann et al. Sec. 1, 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12956) [full text, partly read]
- Representation collapse + repetition bias (cosine similarity of hidden states >0.9, repeated single-token outputs) is the characteristic internal/output signature of the plateau in algorithmic transformers and in early Pythia/OLMo training — [Gopalani & Hu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688) [full text]
- Post-LN specific: exploding early update, then rising LN input norm, then vanishing gradient through LN and a stuck model (DeepNet Sec. 3); decoder-side exponential gradient decay toward shallow layers at LNs (Takase); BERT lower-layer weight-gradient decay (Zhang & He Fig. 1); trained post-LN plateaus with lower-layer gradient norms 4 orders of magnitude below the top (Emadi) — see Q2 citations.
- fp16 dynamic loss scaling defaults: `GradScaler(init_scale=65536.0, growth_factor=2.0, backoff_factor=0.5, growth_interval=2000)` — [PyTorch AMP docs (deprecated torch.cuda.amp.GradScaler signature)](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/amp.html) [automated page summary]. The docs summary does not spell out the growth/backoff rules, which are the standard behaviour (grow by 2x after 2000 clean steps, halve on overflow); I did not verify those rules on the page.
- Adam epsilon vs gradient scale: when the gradient RMS is of the same order as AdamW's eps, the update magnitude shrinks (a damping effect); at the largest scales/LRs in their sweeps gradient RMS fell to around the default eps (1e-8); lowering eps (to 1e-15 at 4.8B params, LR 0.3) improved loss in their setting, while raising it to 1e-6 diverged there — [Wortsman et al. Sec. 3.4, Fig. 11-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) [automated page summary; not independently read]
- BERT's own Adam eps is 1e-6 (BERT/RoBERTa convention); RoBERTa says training was sensitive to this term — [Liu et al. 2019 Sec. 2, 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)

### Inferences
- **Loss scale 2^23 is weak evidence of tiny gradients.** (1) With default settings the scale starts at 2^16 and doubles every 2000 clean steps, so 2^16 to 2^23 takes exactly 7 x 2000 = 14,000 overflow-free steps; with 2 skipped (halved) steps the scale would need ~18,000 steps of growth, which fits a 20k-step run. So Run B's scale trajectory is essentially the maximum growth rate under defaults, and says only "no overflow after the first steps". (2) At scale 2^23 the largest representable scaled gradient (65504) corresponds to an unscaled activation-gradient magnitude of only 65504 / 8,388,608 = 0.0078. (3) HF's masked-LM loss is a mean over labelled tokens (assuming no extra accumulation division), i.e., ~0.15 x 256 x 1000 = 38,400 positions (Run A) or ~19,700 (Run B), so per-logit gradients are bounded by ~1/N, about 2.6e-5 to 5e-5, even in a healthy model. Healthy fp16 BERT training could therefore also reach very high loss scales. The signal is "gradients never exceeded ~0.008 per element in fp16 tensors", not "gradients are tiny". Only measured per-layer gradient norms can answer that (see Q5).
- **Entropy collapse is the least likely match**: Zhai et al. tie it to high gradient norms, oscillation or divergence, mostly at high LR; our runs show flat loss and (apparently) no overflow at LR 3.75e-5 to 1e-4. Not excluded for individual heads (needs per-head entropy).
- **Rank/representation collapse + small Q/K gradients is a plausible match** for "unigram-like predictions with context ignored" (top guess "the" even for "the cat sat on the [MASK] ."), and Noci's analysis predicts that depth and uniform-attention (long sequences) make it worse; Gopalani & Hu additionally predict hidden-state cosine similarity >0.9 on the plateau. This is the hypothesis diagnostics should test first.
- **Post-LN lower-layer gradient vanishing is plausible but contested for encoders** (Q2). A concrete interaction is worth testing: with eps = 1e-6, once lower-layer gradients become small the Adam update is no longer sign-like. For a BERT-large layer (~12.6M weights), a block gradient norm of 7e-6 (Emadi's plateaued-run value, a different model) corresponds to a per-element RMS of ~2e-9, i.e., ~500x below eps 1e-6, so those weights would move ~1/500 as far per step as Adam normally would at the same LR; for the whole 3.3e8-parameter model, a global gradient norm below ~0.02 (about 1e-6 x sqrt(3.3e8)) means the *average* parameter's gradient RMS is below eps. This would create a self-reinforcing trap (tiny gradients -> tiny updates -> no escape) that Adam's usual scale-invariance does not protect against. This is an inference from Wortsman's damping statement plus arithmetic, not shown in any paper for BERT.
- The two most informative contrasts available from the signature itself: Run A's 5.62 final loss is well below the 7.42 unigram cross-entropy, so the model is not a pure unigram predictor; it has learned some context-dependent information while its argmax stays dominated by "the". That fits a partial-solution/repetition-bias plateau better than a fully dead network.

### Gaps
- No paper reports the signature "MLM loss plateau + loss scale growth + unigram-like predictions" together; the diagnostic match is by analogy.
- The effect of fp16 autocast vs bf16 on MLM plateaus is not studied in any source I found (Wortsman et al. used bf16 only; Zhai kept attention operands in fp32).
- Whether eps=1e-6 specifically contributes to BERT plateaus is untested in the sources; Geiping & Goldstein report no noticeable change from varying eps in 1e-6 (with beta2 0.999) in their pre-LN setup, which does not address a post-LN plateau.

## Key Question 4: Which interventions are supported by evidence (pre-LN, LR/warmup, sequence-length warmup, dropout, init scaling, LAMB, clipping, bf16 vs fp16), and what are concrete recommendations for a 24-layer BERT trained from scratch?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence for MLM plateaus/instability favours (1) giving a post-LN run enough steps at undecayed LR, and (2) switching to pre-LN or a NormFormer-style/B2T/DeepNorm-style variant; for the specific "stuck on the plateau" failure, attention-targeted fixes (temperature/NormSoftmax, qk-norm) have the closest mechanistic support; LAMB, clipping changes, dropout removal and fp16/bf16 have little or no direct evidence for plateau escape.

### Cited Findings

**Architecture / normalization**
- Pre-LN BERT-base trained without warmup at LR 3e-4 where post-LN diverged, converging faster — [Xiong et al. Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745) [full text]. BERT-large with pre-LN, LR 2e-3, 6% warmup, batch 4096 was the best 24-hour configuration, versus 1e-4/11.1% warmup/batch 256 for the reference BERT-large — [Izsak et al. Sec. 3.1, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07705) [full text]. A single-GPU one-day BERT recipe uses pre-LN, peak LR 1e-3 one-cycle, batch 4096, no dropout, seq 128 and finds pre-LN is required for the aggressive schedule — [Geiping & Goldstein Sec. 4.2-4.3, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14034) [full text]
- Megatron's LN rearrangement for BERT beyond BERT-large — [Shoeybi et al. Sec. 5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053) [full text]
- NormFormer-style extra LNs (plus an initialization that scales feed-forward weights down with depth, following Nguyen & Salazar and Bajaj et al.) performed best for BERT-base-size BNC training; ablating FF init scaling changed the reported scores by only about -0.3 to +0.1 points in Table 2 (i.e., a marginal effect in their setting); standard post-norm and pre-norm were both substantially worse and less stable there. Also uses LAMB (LR 0.01, beta2 0.98, eps 1e-6, clip 2.0, 500 warmup steps) because AdamW "behaves poorly" in their highly parallel (32k-batch) regime — [Samuel et al. Sec. 4, 7.1, Table 2, Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09859) [full text, partly read]
- B2T connection (extra residual path skipping all LNs except the last in each layer) makes 18L-18L post-LN trainable and outperforms pre-LN in their text-generation experiments (machine translation among them) — [Takase et al. Sec. 4-6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00330) [full text]

**Initialization / scaling (keep post-LN)**
- DeepNorm: x_{l+1} = LN(alpha * x_l + G(x_l)), with FFN weights and attention value/output projections scaled by beta at init; for encoder-only (BERT-like) models alpha = (2N)^(1/4), beta = (8N)^(-1/4) with N layers; reported to stabilise 1,000-layer NMT models and to bound the early model update. I found NMT results only; no BERT MLM experiment in the text I read — [Wang et al. Sec. 3-4, App. C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00555) [full text]
- Admin adaptive initialization lets 18-layer post-LN converge and outperform 18-layer pre-LN in NMT — [Liu et al. 2020 Sec. 4-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08249) [full text]. T-Fixup claims stable training without warmup or LayerNorm in NMT, up to 200-layer encoder/decoder — [Huang et al., ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/huang20f.html) [abstract only]
- Depth-dependent residual scaling (alpha ~ 1/sqrt(L)) prevents token alignment growth with depth in post-LN at initialization — [Noci et al. Sec. 3.2, Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03126)
- Caution: in a toy shallow setting, smaller init scale made the plateau longer (cosine similarity ~1.0 throughout) — [Gopalani & Hu Fig. 36](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688)

**Attention-targeted**
- NormSoftmax / temperature schedule: faster, more reliable escape in synthetic tasks and an earlier drop for 12-layer RoBERTa on Wikipedia — [Hoffmann et al. Sec. 4-5, Fig. 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12956) [full text, partly read]
- Attention-map bias toward the target pattern shortens plateaus (toy) — [Gopalani & Hu Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688); attention-guidance loss gives faster convergence — [Deshpande & Narasimhan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02399) [abstract only]
- Promoting SAS throughout training (BERT-SAS+) gives early gains but worse later quality; briefly suppressing SAS early improves later quality — [Chen et al. Sec. 4.2-4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311) [full text]
- qk-layernorm lets models train stably across three orders of magnitude of LR (attention-logit growth hypothesis; GPT-style, bf16) — [Wortsman et al. Fig. 1, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) [automated page summary]. sigma-Reparam prevents attention entropy collapse and stabilises post-LN models up to 100L-100L (NMT), removing warmup/adaptive-optimizer need in some tasks; compatible with causal LMs; no BERT MLM test — [Zhai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06296) [full text]

**LR, warmup, optimizer, sequence length, dropout, precision**
- LR and warmup: for post-LN, longer warmup alone did not fix an 18-layer model (Liu 2020, Sec. 5.2); LR 3e-4 diverged post-LN BERT-base (Xiong); warmup 0% diverged for pre-LN BERT-large (Izsak); in Wortsman et al. (GPT-style, default warmup 5k steps) a longer warmup reduces LR sensitivity, most clearly for larger models [automated summary]. RoBERTa-large (post-LN) used 30k warmup at LR 4e-4.
- Sequence-length warmup: stabilises GPT-2/GPT-3-style training at high LR/batch (8x batch, 4x LR on GPT-2; 40x LR on GPT-3 125M) by reducing gradient-variance outliers; best starting lengths were small (8-64 tokens); results are about spikes/divergence, not plateaus — [Li et al. Abstract, Sec. 4-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06084) [full text]. BERT/LTG-BERT: 128 tokens for 90% of steps then 512 — [Devlin et al. App. A.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805); [Samuel et al. Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09859)
- Optimizer: RoBERTa used beta2 0.98, eps 1e-6 and no gradient clipping for BERT-large — [Liu et al. 2019 Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692); LTG-BERT used clip 2.0 with LAMB — [Samuel et al. Table 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09859); LAMB's published benefit is large-batch BERT-large scaling (76 minutes, batch up to 32k-64k), not plateau escape — [You et al., ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00962) [full text, partly read]. Wortsman et al. recommend lowering AdamW eps when gradient RMS falls toward it — [Wortsman et al. Sec. 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) [automated summary]
- Dropout: no plateau evidence. Geiping & Goldstein remove it for single-epoch training — [Sec. 4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14034). Original BERT/RoBERTa/Izsak all use 0.1.
- fp16 vs bf16: no source found testing precision against plateaus. Megatron-LM trained its BERT models (up to 3.9B) with mixed precision and dynamic loss scaling, batch 1024, LR 1e-4 with 10,000 warmup iterations and linear decay, so fp16 loss scaling is not by itself a known cause of BERT stalls — [Shoeybi et al. Sec. 4-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053) [full text, partly read].

### Inferences

**Ranked recommendations for a 24-layer BERT from scratch** (ranking is my judgement from the evidence above; none of these has been tested on our setup):
1. **Cheapest, best-supported test of the "still on the plateau" hypothesis: continue Run B (or restart from its checkpoint) at constant peak LR (no decay) for well beyond 30K steps (e.g., 50-80K).** Evidence: Chen's BERT-base escaped at 20-30K steps with decay negligible (1M-step schedule); decay-to-zero schedules (both our runs) remove step size just where escape would occur. If a drop occurs, the failure was truncation; if not, move to item 2.
2. **Switch to pre-LN with a final LayerNorm, or a NormFormer-style variant, and raise LR (3e-4 to 1e-3 range; pre-LN BERT-base 3e-4 no warmup in Xiong; 1e-3 in Cramming; 2e-3 in Izsak at batch 4096).** Strongest direct evidence for BERT-style MLM (Xiong, Izsak, Cramming, Megatron). Caveat: both pre-LN and NormFormer-like results come from different batch sizes/sequence lengths/wall-clock budgets; pre-LN can slightly degrade final quality in some regimes (Nguyen & Salazar; Samuel et al.).
3. **If post-LN must be kept** (e.g., to stay comparable with a prior baseline): DeepNorm-style init and residual scaling for N = 24: alpha = (2x24)^(1/4) = 2.63 on the residual, beta = (8x24)^(-1/4) = 0.269 applied to FFN weights and the attention value and output projections at initialization (derived from the DeepNet encoder-only formulas; HF BERT's default init is N(0, 0.02), which for width 1024 is already close to Xavier scale (sqrt(2/(1024+4096)) = 0.0198 for the FFN matrices), so beta would shrink them by ~3.7x). Alternatively Admin or B2T. Evidence is NMT-only; expect to need a pilot.
4. **Add an attention-gradient safeguard**: qk-layernorm or NormSoftmax/temperature scaling (Hoffmann; Wortsman). Direct evidence of earlier escape exists only for a 12-layer RoBERTa at small batch; worth a short A/B, not a commitment.
5. **Optimizer hygiene tests**: eps 1e-8 (PyTorch default) vs 1e-6 on a resumed checkpoint; keep beta2 0.98-0.99 (RoBERTa) and grad clip 1.0 (LTG-BERT 2.0; RoBERTa none) — neither is a plausible cause of a plateau at these gradient scales (clipping is inactive when gradients are small); LAMB is a large-batch tool and has no plateau-specific evidence, though its per-layer trust-ratio would rescale small-gradient lower layers (inference).
6. **Warmup/ramp**: for post-LN, use a gentler ramp (original BERT 1e-8 LR per step; RoBERTa-large 1.3e-8) if keeping post-LN at LR 1e-4+; longer warmup alone is not shown to fix deep post-LN (Liu) and not shown to be the cause here.
7. **Sequence length**: a 128-token phase for most of training followed by longer sequences is the standard BERT/LTG-BERT recipe and cuts per-step cost ~4-8x, allowing many more steps in the same compute; evidence that it speeds plateau escape is indirect (SLW for GPT stability; Nagatsuka abstract). Our Run A used 1000 tokens from step 0, longer than any recipe I found for BERT from scratch.
8. **Dropout and precision**: no plateau evidence; keep dropout as dictated by the multi-epoch (30-epoch) data regime, and run a short fp32 or bf16 control from a checkpoint only as a diagnostic.
- **Budget framing** (arithmetic): Chen's 20-30K step escape is ~2.6-3.9B tokens at 131k tokens/step. A 128-token phase at batch 256 would need ~4x the steps to see the same tokens but each step is ~4x cheaper; whether escape is step-limited or token-limited is not established in any source found.

### Gaps
- No direct, controlled study of any single intervention on the plateau of a 24-layer BERT; evidence is a patchwork of NMT, GPT, ViT, 12-layer BERT, and toy-task results.
- DeepNorm has no BERT MLM experiment in the text read; T-Fixup, Nguyen & Salazar, NormFormer, RAdam, Nagatsuka, Deshpande, Belrose were checked at abstract level only; Wortsman and PyTorch AMP details come from automated page summaries.
- No evidence on dropout or on fp16 vs bf16 for plateaus.
- LAMB/AdamW comparisons for deep post-LN plateaus: none found.

## Key Question 5: What diagnostics can be run on existing checkpoints to confirm which failure mode we have?

### Takeaway
Every candidate mechanism has a published measurement that can be applied to existing checkpoints and to short controlled resumes: per-layer gradient norms and RMS relative to Adam eps (vanishing gradients), LN input norms and per-step output change (DeepNet/Liu), token-representation cosine similarity or effective rank per layer (rank collapse), per-head attention entropy (entropy collapse), and attention-pattern/SAS probes (Chen). Each predicts a different outcome, listed below.

### Cited Findings
- Per-layer, per-module gradient norms across depth: Xiong Fig. 3 (FFN parameter gradients per layer), Liu Fig. 3 (relative gradient-norm histograms per sub-layer), Takase Fig. 3-4 (gradient norm per layer and per LN-delimited part), Zhang & He Fig. 1 (weight-gradient norm vs layer id at L = 12/24/48), Emadi Fig. 1d (per-block gradient norms) — see sources above.
- LayerNorm-input norm ||x|| compared with sqrt(d), and the early model-update norm ||F(x, theta_i) - F(x, theta_0)||: DeepNet Fig. 4 [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00555). Output-change test under a single parameter update: Liu et al. Sec. 4 [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08249)
- Token alignment: average cosine of the angle between token representations per layer: Noci Fig. 1 [Noci et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03126); cosine similarity of hidden states plus output repetition frequency and output entropy: Gopalani & Hu Fig. 2, 8 [Gopalani & Hu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688); residual-norm decomposition for rank collapse: Dong Fig. 2 [Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03404)
- Attention entropy per head: Zhai Sec. 3.1 [Zhai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06296); attention to syntactic neighbours via implicit parse UAS (Clark et al. 2019 probe) and BLiMP/n-gram-context probes (masked-token likelihood given fixed n-gram windows): Chen Sec. 2.1, App. A, App. M [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07311)
- Gradient RMS vs AdamW eps and relation to update size: Wortsman Sec. 3.4, Fig. 11-13 [Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) [automated summary]
- Temperature/attention-bias interventions as causal probes: Hoffmann (temperature, NormSoftmax) and Gopalani & Hu (attention-map bias c) [Hoffmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12956), [Gopalani & Hu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688)

### Inferences
Suggested checklist on existing checkpoints (several checkpoints from each run, and an early/late pair from Run B), with what each failure mode predicts; this mapping is my own interpretation of the cited measurements:
1. **Per-layer gradient norms (forward/backward on a fixed batch, in fp32, per parameter tensor; report RMS = norm/sqrt(numel) next to Adam eps 1e-6).**
   - Post-LN lower-layer vanishing (DeepNet/Takase/Emadi): gradient norms increase sharply with layer index (orders of magnitude between layer 0 and 23), and lower-layer RMS falls below ~1e-6 (the eps-trap).
   - Healthy-but-slow plateau (Chen-type): roughly balanced or mildly graded norms, RMS >> eps.
   - Compare W_Q/W_K gradients with W_V/W_O within each layer (Noci Eq. 17-18: rank collapse makes Q/K gradients shrink while V stays normal; Hoffmann Fig. 4: K/Q << V before an escape).
2. **Token representation geometry per layer**: mean pairwise cosine between token states over real batches (and effective rank / top singular-value fraction). Rank/representation collapse: cosine >0.9 in upper layers (Gopalani) or growing with depth (Noci); healthy: markedly lower, more diverse.
3. **Per-head attention entropy (and max attention probability), normalised by log(sequence length)**, at layers across depth, at a few checkpoints. Near-uniform attention (normalised entropy ~1) in most heads supports rank collapse/small Q/K gradient; many heads near zero entropy supports Zhai's entropy collapse; also measure attention to the previous/next token and to [CLS]/[SEP] and the UAS-style implicit-parse probe (Chen) to see whether SAS exists yet. Chen's plateau predicts UAS ~0.05-0.09 on a Wall-Street-Journal sample.
4. **Output-distribution diagnostics on masked positions**: output entropy, top-1 share of "the", repetition/mode fraction, per-position accuracy, and context sensitivity (e.g., KL between predictions on original vs shuffled-context inputs). A unigram-like predictor has near-zero context sensitivity.
5. **Post-LN dynamics check (DeepNet)**: per-layer LayerNorm input RMS versus 1 (post-LN normalises, so measure the pre-LN residual-sum norm vs sqrt(d)), and early-training model-update norm if early checkpoints exist (Run B step 0-2k).
6. **Controlled short resumes from a mid-plateau checkpoint (a few hundred steps, same data order, log loss and the diagnostics above)**: (a) constant LR instead of decay; (b) eps 1e-8; (c) fp32 or bf16 instead of fp16; (d) attention temperature below the default (Hoffmann's "Heat Treatment") or qk-norm; (e) no dropout. A change that moves loss and per-layer gradient norms identifies the active mechanism. Interpreting (a): if only a longer/larger-LR run helps, it supports the Chen-type "needs more steps" reading; if (b) lifts lower-layer updates, eps-trap; if (d) helps, attention-gradient bottleneck.
7. Cheap sanity check on the loss-scale signal: log the unscaled global gradient norm per step and the maximum absolute fp16 gradient seen (see Q3 arithmetic); the loss-scale value alone cannot discriminate.

### Gaps
- No source gives pass/fail thresholds for these diagnostics in a 24-layer BERT; the predicted values above are interpretations of the cited papers' qualitative findings, not measured baselines.
- Whether our existing checkpoints include the early steps (0-4k) needed for the model-update and LN-input-norm diagnostics is unknown from here (and Run A/B checkpoint spacing is not given in the assignment).
