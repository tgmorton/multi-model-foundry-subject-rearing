# Canonical BERT-large-class MLM pretraining recipes vs. our bert_large-en-baseline-h0-s137 recipe

Scope: primary-source audit of from-scratch MLM pretraining recipes (BERT, RoBERTa, Megatron-BERT, NVIDIA LAMB BERT-large, ELECTRA's BERT baselines, DeBERTa, MosaicBERT, ALBERT, plus budget/small-data recipes: 24hBERT, Cramming, ModernBERT, LTG-BERT, BabyLM RoBERTa) and what they report about MLM loss trajectories.

Conventions: "loss" = masked-token cross-entropy in nats (perplexity = exp(loss)). arXiv papers were read from the PDF text locally (pdftotext); GitHub code/README facts were fetched with WebFetch (small-model extraction of verbatim snippets) and are marked "(code)". Numbers marked "~fig" were read by eye off a figure and are approximate. Inferences are only in the Inferences subsections.

Our run for reference (from the task brief): 24L/1024/16H post-LN HF BertForMaskedLM, vocab 50,004, 1000-token contiguous chunks with no [CLS]/[SEP], 15% 80/10/10, AdamW (0.9, 0.99, eps 1e-6, wd 0.01 on all params), peak LR 3.75e-5, warmup 976 steps (6.6%), linear decay to 0 over 14,820 steps, batch 256 x 1000 = 256K tokens/step, ~3.8B tokens (30 epochs of ~120M tokens), dropout 0.058/0.021, fp16 autocast + GradScaler, clip 1.0. Result: train loss 5.62, held-out masked loss 6.51 (unigram CE 7.42), accuracy 13.8%. Rater probe: lr 1e-4, warmup 6%, beta2 0.98, dropout 0.1, seq 512, batch 256x512, 20k steps (2.6B tokens), loss ~6.1 from step 4k to 20k.

---

## Q1. Exact canonical recipes (peak LR, warmup, schedule, batch, seq length, steps, Adam, WD, dropout, precision, LN placement) vs. ours

### Takeaway
Every canonical 24-layer post-LN-style recipe uses a peak LR of 1e-4 or higher (1e-4 at batch 256-1024; 2e-4 to 4e-4 at batch 2K-8K; LAMB 1.76e-3 to 6e-3 at 4K-64K), with warmup of 6% (RoBERTa/MosaicBERT) or 10,000 steps, and very long horizons (125K to 2M steps) except the deliberately budget-tuned recipes (24hBERT, Cramming, LTG-BERT, LAMB), which compensate for few steps with much higher LR (1e-3 to 1e-2) and pre-/NormFormer-LN. Our 3.75e-5 peak at 14.8K total steps is below every pretraining peak found (it sits in the range the BERT paper recommends for fine-tuning); our other settings (eps 1e-6, 15%/80-10-10, wd 0.01, clip 1.0, GELU, 24L/1024/4096/16) match canonical values.

Additional optimizer-implementation facts:
- Original BERT code's Adam omits bias correction (update = m / (sqrt(v) + eps)), applies 0.01 decay only to non-LayerNorm, non-bias parameters, and clips global gradient norm to 1.0 — [google-research/bert optimization.py (code)](https://github.com/google-research/bert/blob/master/optimization.py).
- fairseq's Adam (used for RoBERTa) applies `p.add_(p, alpha=-weight_decay*lr)` to every parameter it is given, has no built-in exclusion for LayerNorm/bias, and uses bias correction — [fairseq adam.py (code)](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairseq/blob/main/fairseq/optim/adam.py). Whether the RoBERTa param groups excluded LN/bias is not shown in what I fetched, and the RoBERTa paper does not state it.
- HF `Trainer` excludes `bias` and LayerNorm weights from weight decay by default (decay only for params whose names are not in the LayerNorm set and do not contain "bias") — [transformers v4.41.2 trainer.py (code)](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v4.41.2/src/transformers/trainer.py).
- NVIDIA's LAMB pipeline excludes `['bias', 'gamma', 'beta', 'LayerNorm']` from decay — [run_pretraining.py (code)](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DeepLearningExamples/blob/master/PyTorch/LanguageModeling/BERT/run_pretraining.py).
- RoBERTa: "we found training to be very sensitive to the Adam epsilon term, and in some cases we obtained better performance or improved stability after tuning it. Similarly, we found setting beta2 = 0.98 to improve stability when training with large batch sizes." — [RoBERTa Sec 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11692).
- Cramming: "We find no noticeable change in varying these parameters in reasonable amounts, e.g. eps = 1e-6, beta1 = 0.9, beta2 = 0.999" — [Cramming Sec 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14034).

#### Architecture constants that match
- BERT-large dimensions: L=24, H=1024, A=16, 340M params — [BERT Sec 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04805); FFN 4096, head size 64 — [RoBERTa Table 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11692), [ELECTRA Table 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10555), [DeBERTa Table 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.03654). GELU, dropout 0.1 "on all layers" — [BERT A.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04805). eps 1e-6 appears in BERT-family Adam settings of BERT/RoBERTa/ELECTRA/DeBERTa/MosaicBERT/LTG-BERT/Izsak (rows above). So our model shape, GELU, MLM objective, eps, wd 0.01, clip 1.0 are canonical.

#### Post-LN vs pre-LN evidence in the same recipes
- Megatron: "rearranging the order of the layer normalization and the residual connections ... is critical to enable the scaling of the BERT-style models beyond BERT-Large. The architecture (b) ... eliminates instabilities observed using the original BERT architecture in (a) and also has a lower training loss"; Fig. 7 caption: "While the original architecture performs well on the 336M model, the modifications in (b) enable stable training with lower training loss" — [Megatron Sec 5.3, Fig 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08053). I.e., original post-LN is the canonical choice for the BERT-large size we use.
- Xiong et al.: for Post-LN "the expected gradients of the parameters near the output layer are large. Therefore, using a large learning rate on those gradients makes the training unstable. The warm-up stage is practically helpful"; for BERT-base on Wikipedia+BookCorpus they "follow the same hyper-parameter configuration in Devlin et al. to train the Post-LN BERT using 10k warmup steps with lrmax = 1e-4" and "have tried to use a larger learning rate (such as 3e-4) for the Post-LN BERT but found the optimization diverged", whereas Pre-LN BERT trains from 3e-4 with no warmup — [Xiong et al. 2020, Abstract & Sec 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.04745).
- Liu et al. (Admin): "Pre-LN converges in all 15 settings, and Post-LN diverges in 7 out of 15 settings; when Post-LN converges, it outperforms Pre-LN in 7 out of 8 settings" (WMT'14 translation, not BERT) — [Liu et al. 2020, Sec 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.08249).
- Cramming: "the key effect of pre-normalization is to stabilize training and enable larger learning rates and reduced warmup" — [Cramming Sec 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14034). Izsak/24hBERT also use pre-LN ("with pre-layer normalization (Shoeybi et al., 2019)") — [Izsak Sec 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07705).
- LTG-BERT: "Both the post-norm and pre-norm transformer variants perform substantially worse than the NormFormer-like layer normalization. Both of them also lead to less stable and only slightly faster training" — [LTG-BERT Sec 7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.09859).

### Inferences
- Our LR is the single hyperparameter that is outside every canonical pretraining range found; every other scalar (eps, wd, clip, dropout order of magnitude, 15% masking, post-LN, GELU, shape) matches or is immaterial.
- In token-per-step terms our 256K tokens/step is ~2x BERT's 131K (len 512) and ~8x BERT's 32K (len 128); the closest canonical batch in *tokens* is Chen's/Devlin's 256x512, which both use LR 1e-4 (3x ours). Under fairseq's own LR-vs-batch table (256 -> 1e-4, 2048 -> 5e-4) a batch of ~500 length-512 sequences (our token count) would be ~1.5e-4 to 2e-4, i.e. ~4-5x above 3.75e-5.
- Total optimizer steps is where we are an outlier: 14,820 steps versus 1M (BERT), 500K (RoBERTa-large), 2M (Megatron), 1M (DeBERTa), 464K (ELECTRA BERT-Large), 178K (MosaicBERT-Large), 125K (ALBERT), 70K (MosaicBERT-Base), 31K (RoBERTa Table 3 bsz 8K), 31K (LTG-BERT), ~23K (24hBERT), 8.6K (LAMB 64K/32K). The only recipes in our step range are the ones that use LR 1e-3 to 1e-2 and pre-/NormFormer-LN (24hBERT, LTG-BERT, Cramming) or LAMB (NVIDIA, LAMB paper).

### Gaps
- Megatron paper does not state BERT sequence length or whether LN/bias are excluded from weight decay; only the repo example (seq 512) was found.
- NVIDIA README/code fetched do not expose LAMB betas/eps/clip (they use `FusedLAMBAMP` defaults not shown); vocabulary size and LN placement are not stated in the README text I got (original BERT architecture = post-LN by lineage, not by an NVIDIA statement). Note a source discrepancy: LAMB paper describes decay eta0*(1-t/T) (power 1), NVIDIA's code default is `degree=0.5`.
- MosaicBERT Table S1 lists "WD 1e-5" for "Decoupled AdamW" (MosaicML Composer); I did not verify whether Composer scales decoupled decay by LR, so the effective per-step decay is uncertain.
- ModernBERT appendix A hyperparameter table (WD, betas, eps) did not extract cleanly; only text-level numbers above were captured.
- MLPerf's BERT reference hyperparameters (LR, batch, initial checkpoint) could not be retrieved: the raw README URL returned 404 and the current training-rules page no longer has BERT rows.
- The BERT paper's "approximately 40 epochs" accounting (1M steps x 256 x 512 tokens = 131B tokens ~ 40 x 3.3B words) ignores that 90% of steps use length 128 (~43B tokens by my arithmetic); I did not resolve this ambiguity.

## Q2. Reported MLM loss / perplexity trajectories; is loss ~5.6-6.1 after 2.6-3.8B tokens possible for a working BERT-large?

### Takeaway
Yes as a transient, no as an endpoint. Three independent primary sources (Chen et al. BERT-base, Izsak et al. BERT-base/large/24hBERT, Cramming) show healthy BERT-style MLM training sitting on a plateau at ~6.0 nats for a stretch (about the first ~20K steps at lr 1e-4 / batch 256x512 = ~2.6B tokens for BERT-base; ~3 h for BERT-large with default HPs in Izsak), then dropping abruptly to ~2-3 within ~10K steps and creeping to ~1.7-2.0; converged BERT-large-class models report 1.2-1.7 nats (perplexity ~3.2-5.5). Our rater probe (6.1 flat from 4k to 20k at lr 1e-4, batch 256x512) matches Chen's pre-drop plateau almost exactly and was stopped at the step where Chen's run was just leaving it; our 14.8K-step production run never gets there.

### Cited Findings

#### Converged / reference values (nats; perplexity = exp)
- BERT Table 6 (held-out training data, masked-LM perplexity, "otherwise using the same hyperparameters and training procedure"): 12L/768/12 heads **3.99** (1.38 nats); 12L/1024/16 **3.54** (1.26); **24L/1024/16 = 3.23 (1.17 nats)**; 6L/768/12 4.68 (1.54); 3L/768/12 5.84 (1.77) — [BERT Table 6, Sec 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04805). Conversions to nats are mine.
- RoBERTa Table 3, BERT-base on BookCorpus+Wikipedia, held-out ppl: **3.99** (bsz 256, lr 1e-4, 1M steps), **3.68** (2K, 7e-4, 125K steps), **3.77** (8K, 1e-3, 31K steps) — [RoBERTa Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11692). (31K steps at 8K batch reaches ppl 3.77 = 1.33 nats.)
- NVIDIA BERT-large LAMB pretraining, "Final Loss" 1.2437 (mixed precision, 32 DGX A100 nodes) and 1.2465 (TF32); stability test mean 1.265, std 0.023 (seeds 1.260, 1.265, 1.304, 1.256, 1.242) — [NVIDIA README, Training accuracy results](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DeepLearningExamples/blob/master/PyTorch/LanguageModeling/BERT/README.md). This loss is MLM + NSP summed ("total_loss = masked_lm_loss + next_sentence_loss") — [run_pretraining.py (code)](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DeepLearningExamples/blob/master/PyTorch/LanguageModeling/BERT/run_pretraining.py).
- Megatron-BERT 336M / 1.3B / 3.9B: validation perplexity **1.58 / 1.30 / 1.16** on a 3% held-out set — [Megatron Sec 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08053). Not comparable: whole-word n-gram masking + SOP.
- MLPerf Training BERT is scored as time to reach **0.72 masked-LM accuracy** with BERT-large on Wikipedia — [MLCommons benchmarks page](https://mlcommons.org/benchmarks/training/); "converges to 72% MLM accuracy on the evaluation data set" — [Unpadded BERT / MLPerf v2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.08124).
- 24hBERT (BERT-large, pre-LN, 24 h, lr 2e-3, bsz 4096): validation loss **1.717** (Search #1); BERT-base with default HPs: 2.050; BERT-large with default HPs (bsz 256, lr 1e-4, 11.1% warmup, 8.3-day schedule): **2.318** at 24 h — [Izsak Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07705).
- Cramming (BERT-base-like, pre-LN, 24 h on one GPU, single epoch): "all choices reach a MLM loss around 1.9 at the end of training" — [Cramming Sec 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14034). From Fig. 1 (~fig, log axes): loss ~7 at ~30-50M tokens, ~6 at ~70-100M tokens, ~4 near ~200M tokens, ~3 near ~0.5-0.7B tokens, and ~2.9 -> ~1.9 between 2B and 7B tokens (right panel).
- ALBERT: "even after training for 1M steps, our largest models still do not overfit" and removing dropout "significantly improves MLM accuracy" — [ALBERT Sec 4.x / Fig 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.11942).

#### The plateau at ~6 (healthy runs)
- Chen et al. (BERT-base, uncased, lr 1e-4, 10,000 warmup, batch 256, seq 512): "we instead identify a precipitous drop in the loss curve of BERTBase (Fig. 1(a)), consistently spanning 20K-30K timesteps of training across various random seeds"; "there exists an early apparent phase transition in the MLM loss (at around 6K steps)" — [Chen et al. 2023, Sec 3-4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07311). Reading Fig. 1(a) (~fig): loss starts ~10.3-10.5 (= ln 30,522), falls to ~7 by ~1K steps, then declines only from ~6.5 to ~5.9 over steps ~3K-20K, drops to ~2.5 by ~30K steps and ~1.8 by 100K steps. The drop coincides with the formation of syntactic attention structure (UAS spike) and then BLiMP gains. Their same-recipe MultiBERTs replications show the same shape — [Chen et al. App F](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07311).
- Izsak Fig. 2 (~fig, validation MLM loss vs. wall-clock hours, 24 h budget): BERT-large with default HPs holds at ~6.0-6.1 from ~1 h to ~3.3 h, drops to ~3.5 by ~6 h and ~2.3 at 24 h; BERT-base default holds ~6.0 until ~1.5 h, then drops; 24hBERT (lr 2e-3, bsz 4096, 6% warmup, pre-LN) leaves ~6.0 within ~0.5 h — [Izsak Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07705). The larger model with the same HPs has the longer plateau.
- Izsak pruning rule: "After 3 hours, we prune configurations that did not reach a validation-set loss of 6.0 or less; this rule removes diverging runs, such as configurations with 0% warmup." They also note that the schedule must be tied to the budget: "a clear preference towards synchronizing the learning rate schedule with the actual amount of training time" — [Izsak Sec 4.2-4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07705).
- ModernBERT-large at LR 5e-4: "training loss, validation metrics, and live evaluations on MNLI had plateaued for a few hundred billion tokens"; rolling back to LR 5e-5 and wd 1e-6 resumed progress — [ModernBERT App C.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13663) (a late-training plateau; opposite LR direction, not the early ~6 plateau).
- BERT paper on MLM speed: "The MLM model does converge slightly slower than the LTR model" — [BERT App C.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04805).

### Inferences
- **Is 5.6-6.1 "broken"?** Not by itself. The ~6.0 level is the standard first plateau of BERT-family MLM training in at least three unrelated codebases, vocab 30K-32K, seq 128 and 512. Our rater probe (6.1 from step 4k to 20k, lr 1e-4, batch 256x512 - exactly Chen's batch geometry and LR) is what Chen's healthy BERT-base looks like before its drop at ~20-30K steps (2.6-3.9B tokens at 131K tokens/step); the probe ended at 20K steps = 2.6B tokens, right at the edge, so the probe alone cannot separate "healthy but not yet transitioned" from "stuck".
- **But the endpoint is abnormal.** No canonical run ends a schedule on the plateau; in every source the plateau is a transient of ~0.1B tokens (Cramming), ~0.5 h (24hBERT), ~3 h (BERT-large default HPs), or ~20K steps (BERT-base, lr 1e-4). A converged BERT-large-class model sits at 1.2-1.7 nats (ppl 3.2-5.5; BERT, NVIDIA, Izsak), and MLPerf scores BERT-large on reaching 72% masked-LM accuracy versus our 13.8%. At 2.6-3.8B tokens tuned recipes are already at ~2 nats (Cramming), so token budget alone is not the limitation.
- **Why the production run likely cannot escape (heuristic, my arithmetic).** Adam moves each parameter by roughly lr per step, so cumulative LR (sum of lr over steps) bounds how far parameters can travel. Chen's BERT-base reaches the drop (20K steps) with sum(lr) = 10,000 x 1e-4/2 + 10,000 x 1e-4 = **~1.5** (and ~2.5 by 30K). Ours totals 976 x 3.75e-5/2 + 13,844 x 3.75e-5/2 = **~0.28 for the entire run** (5x less than where a healthy BERT-base escapes; and a 24-layer model escapes later than a 12-layer model per Izsak Fig. 2). The rater probe totals ~1.0 by step 20K (lr 1e-4, 1200-step warmup, linear decay to 0 at 20K): about 0.67x Chen's cumulative LR at the 20K-step drop, with the LR itself decaying to ~0 exactly when a healthy run would be escaping. This is a heuristic, not a measured law, and Chen's model is half our depth.
- Train 5.62 vs held-out 6.51 after 30 epochs: the gap says the model is already fitting the repeated training text (memorization) without having acquired the contextual structure that produces the drop; I found no primary-source value for this gap.
- A reported "predicts the at 56% of masked positions" is consistent with a unigram-like/frequent-token regime; I found no primary source quantifying this behavior on the plateau (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No primary source reports a per-step table of BERT-large MLM loss at billions-of-tokens checkpoints; all plateau/transition values above are read from figures (Chen Fig 1a; Izsak Fig 2; Cramming Fig 1) and are approximate.
- I did not find primary-source plateau values for a 50K vocabulary or for a ~120M-token corpus; the ~6.0 plateau is for ~30K-32K-vocab Wikipedia/Books/C4-style data. Unigram entropy of those corpora is not reported, so I cannot say whether 6.0 is above or below their unigram CE (ours: 7.42).
- Chen et al.'s figure of "100K steps (~13M tokens)" in their Table 1 caption is inconsistent with 256x512 tokens/step (would be ~13B); I used step counts only.
- No source gives GradScaler/loss-scale trajectories (our scale reached 2^23); nothing to compare.
- The MLPerf 0.72 accuracy target is a final-quality threshold for a possibly-warm-started benchmark run; I could not retrieve the reference's initial checkpoint details.

## Q3. Which of our deviations do the primary sources flag as important, and with what evidence?

### Takeaway
Primary sources flag LR/schedule-length (and post-LN's dependence on warmup/LR) as decisive for plateau escape; they report no or negligible effect for no-[CLS]/[SEP], long contiguous sequences, weight decay on LayerNorm/bias, small-corpus repetition (BERT itself ran many epochs; LTG-BERT ~250-1000 epochs on 100M words), and eps/beta2 within normal ranges. Two data-side items get cautionary (not decisive) flags for ~100M-word corpora: a 50K vocabulary (LTG-BERT's rule gives ~16K) and long contexts (a BabyLM study preferred 32-64).

### Inferences
- Ranking of plausibility (most to least) for the stalled loss: (1) LR too low x horizon too short x LR annealed to zero (our production Sigma-lr ~0.28 vs ~1.5 where healthy BERT-base escapes; every canonical recipe is 3-10x higher in LR at comparable batch); (2) the HP sweep itself - trials scored while all were on the ~6.0 plateau cannot discriminate LRs by escape speed, and a plateau-loss criterion can prefer a *lower* LR that hovers slightly lower on the plateau (my inference; not tested); (3) post-LN depth makes the plateau longer for the same HPs (Izsak Fig 2: BERT-large plateau ~2x BERT-base plateau in wall-clock) and caps the usable LR (Xiong), so the fix is a higher LR with long (10K-30K-step or 6%) warmup and a long horizon rather than shorter warmup; (4) 50K vocab / 1000-token contexts on ~120M tokens (cautionary); (5) everything else (beta2, eps, wd placement, special tokens, repetition, fp16, dropout) is within or on the benign side of published practice.
- Weight decay placement is numerically negligible here: decoupled decay shrinks weights by exp(-wd x sum(lr)) = exp(-0.01 x 0.278) ~ 0.9972 over the whole run (my arithmetic), so decaying LayerNorm/bias parameters cannot explain the stall.
- padding_idx: in HF code the `[CLS]` (id 0) row starts at zero and is excluded from embedding-lookup gradient (my reading of PyTorch `padding_idx` semantics; not fetched), but it is also the tied decoder row and so still receives output-layer gradients; since our data never contains `[CLS]` as an input and attention masks handle the real `[PAD]` (id 4, an ordinary trainable row), I expect no first-order effect on loss. No source tests this.
- The relevant LR-vs-batch lever for a ~15K-step budget is the one RoBERTa/LAMB/Izsak/LTG-BERT all pull: raise LR (and batch) together. Within post-LN, RoBERTa-large (4e-4 at 8K), ELECTRA/DeBERTa-large (2e-4 at 2K), MosaicBERT-large (2e-4 at 4K) show the viable ceiling is ~2e-4 to 4e-4 at 2K-8K batch; at batch 256 sequences the canonical value is 1e-4, so a 1e-4 to 2e-4 peak with a long warmup is the evidence-supported region for post-LN BERT-large at our batch.

### Gaps
- No primary source ablates weight decay on LayerNorm/bias, padding_idx handling, or contiguous no-special-token packing for BERT-large; conclusions there rest on absence of flags plus Cramming's cls/sep ablation (base size, 128 seq).
- No primary source studies 1000-token contexts for MLM on ~100M-word corpora; Edman & Bylinina's context result is BLiMP-based on RoBERTa-base and not MLM-loss-based.
- The sweep-design inference (plateau-loss criterion biases toward low LR) is not supported by any source I found.

## Q4. Is peak LR 3.75e-5 (or 1e-4) at batch 256 within the literature range for 24-layer post-LN BERT?

### Takeaway
1e-4 at batch 256 is exactly the canonical value (BERT-Large, DeBERTa's batch-256 ablation, Chen/MultiBERTs, BabyLM RoBERTa-base, fairseq's batch-256 recommendation); 3.75e-5 is ~2.7x below it and below every pretraining peak found, within the range BERT recommends for fine-tuning. Note that "1e-4 at 256 sequences" is canonical only together with a 10,000-step warmup and a 1M-step horizon in which the ~6.0 plateau lasts ~20K steps.

### Cited Findings
- BERT-Large: peak 1e-4, batch 256, 10K warmup, 1M steps — [BERT App A.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04805); "The learning rate we used in the paper was 1e-4" — [google-research/bert README](https://github.com/google-research/bert).
- Batch-256 recipes at 1e-4: DeBERTa-base ablation (lr 1e-4, batch 256, 1M steps) — [DeBERTa Table 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.03654); Chen/MultiBERTs BERT-base (lr 1e-4, 10K warmup, batch 256, seq 512) — [Chen Sec 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07311); RoBERTa Table 3 (bsz 256, lr 1e-4) — [RoBERTa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11692); BabyLM RoBERTa-base (lr 1e-4, batch 256, warmup 10000, 50 epochs) — [Edman & Bylinina](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01955); fairseq README: 256 -> 0.0001, 2048 -> 0.0005, 8192 -> 0.0007 — [fairseq README.pretraining.md](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairseq/blob/main/examples/roberta/README.pretraining.md).
- Larger-batch 24-layer peaks: Megatron-BERT 336M 1e-4 at 1024 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08053)); ELECTRA BERT-Large baseline 2e-4 at 2048; DeBERTa-large 2e-4 at 2k; RoBERTa-large 4e-4 at 8k; MosaicBERT-Large 2e-4 at 4096; ALBERT LAMB 1.76e-3 at 4096; NVIDIA LAMB 6e-3 at 65,536 (phase 1) — see Table A.
- Budget recipes with fewer steps: Izsak 1e-3/2e-3 at 4096-8192 (pre-LN); Cramming 1e-3 (pre-LN); LTG-BERT LAMB 1e-2 initial (NormFormer-LN) — Table A.
- Lower-than-1e-4 values in the BERT literature are fine-tuning LRs: "Learning rate (Adam): 5e-5, 3e-5, 2e-5" — [BERT App A.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04805); ELECTRA GLUE fine-tune "5e-5 for Large" — [ELECTRA Table 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10555); separately, Xiong et al. report Post-LN BERT-base trains at 1e-4 (10k warmup) and diverged at 3e-4 ([Xiong Sec 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.04745)), so the post-LN upper edge for a base-size model is between 1e-4 and 3e-4 at Devlin's batch size.
- Upper-edge warnings: ModernBERT-large at 5e-4 plateaued late and was restarted at 5e-5 ([App C.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13663)); LTG-BERT's 0.01 LAMB LR diverged in some runs ([Table 2 caption](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.09859)); Izsak (pre-LN): 0%-warmup configurations diverged ([Sec 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07705)).

### Inferences
- 3.75e-5 is outside the literature range for pretraining and 1e-4 is at the conservative canonical edge; both are tied to batch 256 *sequences*. Our production run's 256 x 1000 = 256K tokens/step is ~2x the tokens in BERT's 256 x 512 batch, so the batch-matched canonical LR is >= 1e-4, plausibly 1.4e-4 to 2e-4 by fairseq's batch-scaling table; 3.75e-5 is ~3-5x below that.
- The rater probe's 1e-4 is in range, but its linear decay to zero over 20K steps removes the very LR a canonical run still has (~1e-4) at the 20K-30K-step transition (Chen), so it tests less of the canonical trajectory than its peak LR suggests.

### Gaps
- No source reports a *successful* from-scratch BERT-large pretraining at peak LR below 1e-4 at batch 256; absence of evidence (not a demonstration that it cannot work given enough steps).
- Warmup/LR interaction for post-LN specifically at LR > 1e-4 with batch 256-512 on BERT-large was not found in a primary source (Xiong's BERT experiments are base-size; their reported divergence at 3e-4 does not say what warmup length was used for that attempt).
